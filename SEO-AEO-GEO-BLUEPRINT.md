**Brand:** Cleaning Weekly  
**Positioning:** Affordable, simple, local recurring cleaning — not gig-economy, not corporate chain  
**Primary geo entity:** Central Florida (Orlando, Winter Park, Kissimmee, Sanford, Apopka, Oviedo + surrounding communities)  
**Primary audiences:** Homeowners, apartment owners, property managers, office / small-business owners  
**Primary CTA:** Get a free quote · **Secondary:** Call or text

### 1.3 Cluster Detail

#### Cluster A — Recurring Home & Apartment Trust

**Transactional / commercial keywords (3)**
1. `weekly house cleaning Orlando`
2. `recurring apartment cleaning near me Central Florida`
3. `affordable maid service Winter Park FL` *(map to brand language: “weekly home cleaning,” not “maid” in H1 — use as match term)*

**Informational long-tail questions (3)**
1. How much does weekly house cleaning cost in Orlando?
2. Is a local cleaning company better than a gig cleaning app?
3. Do I need to be home during a recurring house cleaning?

**Intent gap (what competitors miss)**  
Generic articles list “dust, vacuum, mop” and vague “starting at $X.” Experts know: **frequency economics** (weekly vs biweekly soil load), **entry protocol after visit one**, **same-team retention rate**, **flat-rate vs hourly surprise fees**, and **what is *not* included** (inside oven, laundry, windows) so trust is built before the quote. Competitors also fail to geo-anchor checklists to Central Florida housing stock (tile/grout humidity, pet hair, vacation-home dust cycles).

---

#### Cluster B — Commercial / Office Reliability

**Transactional / commercial keywords (3)**
1. `weekly office cleaning Orlando`
2. `small business commercial cleaning Central Florida`
3. `after hours office cleaning Kissimmee`

**Informational long-tail questions (3)**
1. How do office cleaning companies price by square footage?
2. Should office cleaning happen before open or after close?
3. What should a weekly office cleaning checklist include for a small suite?

**Intent gap**  
Competitors sell “janitorial packages” without **restroom density math**, **multi-suite coordination**, **key/fob chain of custody**, or **single-invoice vs marketplace markup**. Experts publish: sqft × restroom count × frequency matrices, after-hours SLA windows, and what “workstation wipe-down” actually means (keyboard vs desk perimeter).

**Transactional / commercial keywords (3)**
1. `move out cleaning Orlando landlords`
2. `Airbnb turnover cleaning Central Florida`

**Informational long-tail questions (3)**
1. What does a landlord move-out cleaning checklist include for deposit return?
2. How long should an Airbnb turnover take between checkout and check-in?

**Intent gap**

**Transactional / commercial keywords (3)**
1. `post construction cleaning Orlando`
2. `deep cleaning service Winter Park FL`

**Informational long-tail questions (3)**
1. When should post-construction cleaning happen relative to final inspection?

## PHASE 2: Information Gain Content Engine

**Target cluster:** Cluster A — Recurring Home & Apartment Trust  
**Goal:** Beat scraped “top 10 house cleaning tips” pages with first-hand operational data that LLMs and humans treat as primary-source.

### 2.1 Three Proprietary Data Injections (E-E-A-T)

#### Injection 1 — Central Florida Recurring Pricing Matrix (First-Party Quote Log)

**What to publish:** Anonymized, rolling 90-day quote → booked rate table by:

| Variable | Bins |
|----------|------|
| Bedrooms | 1–2 / 3 / 4+ |
| Bathrooms | 1 / 2 / 3+ |
| Sqft | 500–1200 / 1201–2000 / 2001–3500 / 3500+ |
| Frequency | Weekly / Biweekly / Monthly |
| City band | Orlando core / Winter Park–Oviedo / Kissimmee–Sanford–Apopka |

**Deliverable format:** Interactive or static table + methodology footnote (“n = X booked quotes, Qx 2026”).  
**Mock seed metrics (replace with live data):** e.g. median weekly 3bd/2ba ~1,800 sqft Orlando = **$129–$159/week**; biweekly premium soil factor **+18–25% per visit** vs weekly maintenance rate.

**Why it proves experience:** Only operators with a real quote engine and booking log can publish this. Scraped blogs invent round numbers.

---

#### Injection 2 — Same-Team Continuity & Skip Protocol Architecture

**What to publish:** Exact operational workflow as a technical architecture diagram + metrics:

1. Visit 0: walkthrough / entry instructions captured  
2. Visits 1–4: preference learning window  
3. Steady state: named team assignment  
4. Skip/pause: **48-hour notice**, no penalty for reasonable changes  
5. Substitution rule: when primary team unavailable → briefed backup with preference sheet  

**Mock case metrics (replace with live ops KPIs):**

- Same-team continuity rate: **≥ 85% of visits** in a 12-week window  
- First-90-day churn when continuity &lt; 70%: **2.1× higher** than high-continuity accounts  
- Skip utilization: **~1.4 skips / household / quarter** without cancellation  

**Why it proves experience:** Gig apps cannot promise continuity; national chains rarely publish substitution SOPs. This is operator-grade process IP.

---

#### Injection 3 — Inclusion / Exclusion Scope Ledger (Anti-Surprise Contract)

**What to publish:** A machine-readable scope ledger (also human FAQ) mapping every recurring visit:

| Always included | Never included (unless add-on) | Conditional |
|-----------------|--------------------------------|-------------|
| Kitchen counters, sinks, appliance fronts | Inside oven / fridge deep scrub | Pet-hair extra pass |
| Bath fixtures, mirrors, floors | Laundry / dishes | Inside microwave (deep only) |
| Vacuum + mop main living areas | Exterior windows | Ceiling fans (deep) |
| Dust reachable surfaces + baseboards | Garage / patio | Inside cabinets (move-out) |
| Main trash emptied | Hazardous / biohazard | After-party reset |

Attach **photo examples** of “done” vs “not in scope” for Central Florida tile bathrooms and open-plan kitchens.

**Why it proves experience:** Generic articles say “we clean everything.” Experts reduce chargeback risk by publishing boundaries. This is high information gain for AI Overviews answering “what’s included in weekly cleaning.”

### 2.2 How This Contradicts / Improves Generic Web Consensus

| Generic consensus on the web | Cleaning Weekly information gain |
|------------------------------|----------------------------------|
| “House cleaning costs $100–$200” with no method | Geo-banded, bedroom/bath/sqft/frequency matrix from real quotes |
| “Hire bonded & insured cleaners” (badge spam) | Continuity rate + skip SLA + substitution architecture — trust as **operations**, not badges |
| “Weekly is best for busy families” (vague) | Soil-load economics: biweekly visits cost more *per visit* because dirt compounds; weekly is cheaper to maintain |
| “Apps are convenient and cheaper” | Direct comparison: marketplace take-rate, stranger rotation, surge pricing vs flat local weekly rate |
| “Deep clean = better weekly clean” | Explicit taxonomy: deep = one-time reset → recurring lock-in; different labor hours and checklist |
| “Be home for the cleaner” | Entry-protocol data: majority of recurring clients use lockbox/code after visit one; insurance + background-check framing |

**Net effect for GEO:** When an LLM is asked “How much is weekly cleaning in Orlando?” or “Local cleaner vs Taskrabbit,” the page that supplies **tables, SOPs, and named exclusions** becomes the citation source — not a synonym-stuffed listicle.
